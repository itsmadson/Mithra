Mithra · میترا — an open-source platform that turns street imagery into a road-sign inventory.

Ask a city how many direction signs it owns, where they are, and when each was last seen. Most can't answer. The data exists — it's sitting in panoramic street imagery nobody has counted.

So I built the thing that counts it.

You type a street name. Mithra resolves the real centreline from OpenStreetMap, buffers a corridor around it, walks the imagery along that corridor, and returns every sign it finds — classified, mapped, and attached to the photograph it came from. One boulevard in Mashhad: 132 signs, 4 of 4 tiles, in about six minutes.

The part I care about most is the part that admits what it doesn't know.

Classification runs CLIP zero-shot, and it is confidently wrong about regulatory signs — it will call a pedestrian crossing a guide sign. Rather than hiding that behind a clean number, the dashboard plots the model's confidence distribution against the review threshold, so "the model is guessing" looks like what it is. Anything it isn't sure about goes to a human queue instead of into the count.

Those human judgements then train a replacement classifier — which refuses to be installed unless it beats the model already in service, measured on data it never learned from. A number you can't trace back to a photograph isn't an inventory; it's a rumour with a decimal point.

Built with FastAPI, PostGIS, Next.js, and PyTorch. Fully bilingual — Persian and English, right-to-left and left-to-right — with a palette drawn from Persian visual tradition: lapis, Achaemenid gold, turquoise, desert sand.

Open source under MIT. Docker images published; it runs with one compose command.
